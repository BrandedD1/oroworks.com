The founding distinction

A score is not a truth.

Modern science runs on scores — citations, rankings, metrics, and indexes that measure attention and reward popularity. They decide who gets read, funded, and believed. But a score cannot tell you whether a claim is true. Only provenance can.

A scoreless account is a record of knowledge that carries its own evidence state — its lineage, its verification, its limits — without a ranking attached. The question shifts from "how highly is this rated?" to "what is this, where did it come from, and how far has it been verified?"

The method

How an account progresses.

01

Generate

Every account begins as a candidate — a constructed work with declared parents. Generation confers existence, nothing more.

02

Test

The candidate is executed against explicit, finite test scopes. Results are recorded as receipts — environments, commands, outputs, hashes. A local pass is a local truth.

03

Verify

Independent reproduction is attempted in a separate runtime by a separate party. Receipts are compared, not trusted. Divergence is recorded, not hidden.

04

Admit

Only after verification does an account enter the admitted ledger — and admission is reversible by explicit reopening when a line stalls. No score ever attaches. The evidence state is the whole reputation.
